# Teacher expert brief

> This brief is a **review lens**, not a curriculum conclusion. `domain-expert` reviews the PRD / design / prototype against it; anything the brief does not cover is marked "not covered by the brief" — do not fill the gap by guessing.

## 1. What this profession looks at in this kind of product

- **Learning objectives and curriculum**: are objectives observable, aligned to stage / curriculum standards, with explicit prerequisite knowledge.
- **Cognitive load and pacing**: how much is shown at once, practice spacing, difficulty steps, feedback timing.
- **Assessment alignment**: are practice, instruction and tests the same thing; are items discriminating and explainable.
- **Classroom / home usability**: can a teacher use it in a 40-minute lesson; can a parent understand it; device and network constraints.
- **Accessibility and equity**: fonts / contrast / read-aloud / captions; reachability for students at different levels.
- **Minor protection**: minimal personal data, no spending inducement, no social risk, guardian awareness.

## 2. Review checklist (against PRD / design / prototype)

| # | Focus | What to look for | Basis | Common gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Observable objectives | Is each AC "the student can do X" rather than "the system has Y" | Learning-taxonomy / curriculum standards | Features only, no learning outcome |
| 2 | Stage fit | Vocabulary, examples, UI density appropriate to the stage | Curriculum standards / cognitive development | Adult UI applied to young children |
| 3 | Assessment alignment | Practice, explanation and test share a source; errors explainable | Instructional design principles | Practise A, test B; right/wrong only |
| 4 | Classroom usability | Walk the teacher path: prepare / teach / assign / grade / feedback | Classroom practice | Student side only, teacher side broken |
| 5 | Cognitive load | Information per screen, steps, hint levels | Cognitive-load theory | Crowded screens, no progressive disclosure |
| 6 | Accessibility | Contrast, font size, keyboard / read-aloud / captions | Accessibility standards | Colour-only cues, keyboard-inaccessible |
| 7 | Minors | Collected fields, social / ranking / paid inducement, guardian consent | Minor-protection rules | Public ranking by default, top-up inducement |

## 3. Red lines and licensed boundary

- **Must be confirmed by a curriculum specialist / licensed teacher**: curriculum alignment, item validity, stage appropriateness, special-education needs.
- **AI must not conclude**: never write "meets the curriculum / is effective"; write "principle X requires…, to be confirmed by curriculum review".
- **Cost of missing it**: out-of-scope or age-inappropriate content, unusable in class, distorted assessment, harm to students.

## 4. Common basis (examples; verify the current version)

- Compulsory / upper-secondary curriculum standards (by subject and stage): objectives, content, achievement.
- Instructional design principles (backward design, cognitive-load theory): objective-assessment-activity alignment.
- Minor-protection law / children's personal-information rules: minors' data and spending inducement.
- Accessibility standards (e.g. WCAG): perceivable, operable, understandable.
